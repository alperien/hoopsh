## MEDIUM season-cli.ts:59 - --seed silently reseeds the LEAGUE, so cross-seed reruns compare different rosters under identical team names
`const teams = makeLeague(nTeams, `${seed}:league`)` derives the roster population from the same `--seed` flag that seeds the games (and `--matchup` sims, line 186 `seedBase: `${seed}:mc``). Meanwhile league.ts:133-134 makes team IDENTITY purely positional (`CITIES[k % 18]`, `NICKNAMES[k % 17]` — seed-independent) while the roster, quality offset, and tactics come from the seed-derived Rng (league.ts:132). Consequence: `npm run season -- --matchup 0,3 --seed A` and `--seed B` print the same fixture name ("Aurora Comets vs Delmar Herons") with different players and different strength deltas. A user following the noise-floor doctrine (docs/CALIBRATION.md: "never adjudicate anything from one or two draws — measure more bases") who replicates a matchup estimate across seed bases is silently pooling draws from DIFFERENT populations that look like the same fixture; disagreement reads as engine noise when it is roster change. The honest replication axis (grow `--sims`; per-sim-index seeds make the first n draws reusable, matchup.ts:222-226) is documented, but the seed/population coupling is stated nowhere — not in the CLI header, not in docs/SEASON.md.
Breaks: seed discipline of the matchup measurement instrument — cross-seed comparisons of "the same" fixture are not comparisons of the same fixture; no CLI path exists to hold the league fixed while varying game seeds.

## What is done well
- The roundRobin venue rule is genuinely measured engineering: the doc comment states the property ("|home − away| ≤ 1 within a cycle, ≤ 2 with byes"), names the naive alternative it beat, and the claim survives independent enumeration (this review checked n=2..13, rounds 1/2/3: every pair exactly once per cycle, exact per-building splits under even rounds, no team twice per round label).
- `gameSeed(base, index, home, away)` is a thoughtful seed design: schedule position gives reproducibility, matchup ids buy edit-stability ("tweak the schedule, diff the standings" stays interpretable), and both properties are documented and tested.
- The B3-3 index-coverage guards in runSeason and simulateMatchup (position i must hold index i after the sort) close a real corruption class — an overlapping worker slice with intact count — and both are exercised by adversarial mock seams in the tests.
- The CI math is right and honest: Wilson over Wald with the stated rationale, the power formula reproduces its own rule-of-thumb table (783/194/47/4904, all recomputed here), and the CLI prints an n-sensitivity honesty line so a consumer sees when n was too small instead of trusting a bare point estimate.
- Aggregation discipline: ratio stats are volume-weighted (Σmakes/Σattempts) matching aggregate.ts's convention, with the "never a mean of per-game percentages" trap called out at the type definition, and the two-meanings-of-`pf` reader trap annotated where it lives.
- docs/SEASON.md's "THE DECISION: cross-game state is deliberately absent" section is model honest labeling: what is stateless, where each future piece attaches, and what the omission costs a prediction consumer (HCA magnitude, correlation structure, over-confident season-total tails) — including a self-deprecating record of its own stale-number incident in the margin-noise bullet.
- computeStandings converts the engine's no-ties guarantee into a loud invariant (throws on a tied score) instead of inventing a tiebreak rule, and its sort comparator is a deliberate total order so tied teams stay byte-stable.
- leagues.ts loads NCAA bands from the research deliverable itself rather than a hand copy that could drift, labels NCAA `calibrated: false` so cli.ts defaults it to report-only with an explicit notice, and refuses to resolve EuroLeague until its band research exists.
